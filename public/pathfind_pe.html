Directional Inhibition

Goal-directed competition: clamping two endpoints doesn't inject any activation into the network — it only reshapes who wins the local competitions. Excitation, decay, and external input are bone-stock IAC; the goal information enters purely through the inhibitory weights.

The algorithm

  1. Pools. Every node's neighbors form a competitive pool: a scenario's actions compete to serve it, and an action's scenarios compete to claim it (siblings = nodes sharing a neighbor).
  2. Proximity score. Each pool member is scored by its mean positional similarity to the two clamped endpoints: score = 1 − ‖p − p_clamp‖ / √d, averaged over both clamps. Averaging makes the score peak between the endpoints — the corridor a plan must cross — not just next to one of them.
  3. Relative softmax. Within each pool: α = softmax(score × strength × 10). Because it's a softmax, everything is relative to the pool: the locally best-placed sibling benefits most even if it's globally far from both clamps, and a sibling that's off-target but less off than its rivals is penalized less.
  4. Exemption, not reallocation. Each member's incoming inhibitory weight is scaled by (1 − α) × N/(N−1). The winner's softmax share is subtracted from its own inhibition; nobody else's inhibition is amplified. The pool's total inhibition budget is identical at every strength — the dial only re-aims it.

Worked example

A scenario has three actions with corridor-proximity scores 0.65, 0.60, 0.40. At strength 2 (softmax temperature ×20):

ActionScoreα (softmax)Inhibition received
A (best)0.650.700.45× baseline
B (close 2nd)0.600.261.11× baseline
C (far off)0.400.0051.49× baseline

B is off the ideal line but much closer than C, so it keeps a fighting chance; C absorbs the most competition. Raising strength widens these gaps until only the best member of each pool is effectively exempt.

The strength dial

0 — uniform sibling inhibition (direction-blind baseline). High — each pool's best-placed member becomes immune to its rivals while the rest keep ≈1×, so the winning chain of scenario→action hops survives competition and the activation front follows the corridor between the clamps instead of blooming outward.

Why some off-path neighbors still light up

Excitation itself is undirected: an active node excites all its neighbors through its edges (it cannot choose). Nodes adjacent to the clamps or the path therefore receive real activation — they're the branches the network considered. Directional inhibition decides whose continuation survives, so those considered-but-rejected branches fade rather than propagate. A stronger inhibitory weight prunes them harder.
